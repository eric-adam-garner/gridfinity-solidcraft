Design doc · gridfinity

Gridfinity in SolidCraft: the web generator's bins, baseplates, clips and collections, built as SolidCraft designs that can be extended

A Python package turns the settings of gridfinitygenerator.com into SolidCraft command scripts. One script builds a part headless through solidcraft-cli, writing STL, STEP, 3MF or a SolidCraft design, or replays into the running app as a design of its own. This doc owns what each part is made of, the dimensions it follows and where they depart from the web generator, how the geometry is built in SolidCraft, and where to extend it.

Parts

Four kinds of part are built, each from settings that name the web generator's controls

Every part is set from a JSON file, from command-line options, or both, the options overriding the file. Its settings are checked against the web generator's ranges before anything is built, and a setting out of range stops the build and names the setting.

PartWhat it isCommand
BoxA bin: a foot under every grid cell, a floor, an outer wall with a stacking lip, and walls, ledges and scoops placed insidegridfinity box
BaseplateA pocket for every grid cell, open underneath, with optional magnet and screw pads in the pocket corners, connector slots on the outer edges, and trimming to a drawer's sizegridfinity baseplate
Connection clipThe clip that joins two connectable baseplates across their seamgridfinity clip
CollectionParts laid out by whole cells inside a bounding volume, such as a drawer, each saved as its own design and linked into onegridfinity collection

The web generator's fifth kind, a cutout fitted to an imported STL model, is not built: see what is left out and the open questions.

Settings

A box is set by its grid size, height, lip, holes, walls and elements, with the web generator's ranges and defaults

An element runs along a line of the grid divided into twelfths of a unit. Its start and its line are counted in twelfths from the bin's −X/−Y corner, and its length in twelfths. An element oriented along X starts at x on the line y; one oriented along Y swaps the two. A line on the grid's edge lies on the bin's outer face. A wall is centred on its line. A ledge is a 45 degree shelf 15 mm wide whose top is level with the walls' tops; it hangs from its line toward +Y, or +X for a Y element, and the other way when flipped. A scoop rises from the floor in a curve to its line on the same side. A bin lower than three units takes a shorter ledge and scoop. A bin trimmed to a size keeps the grid's −X/−Y corner; the column or row its trim cuts through stands on narrower feet of the full profile, a foot narrower than its two corners is left out and the floor overhangs there, and an element that lies wholly outside the trimmed bin stops the build. On the command line an element is written X,Y,LENGTH, then y for a Y element and flip to flip it, as in --scoop 0,0,36 or --wall 12,0,24,y.

A collection builds whatever its layout asks for and warns, part by part, where the web generator flags a layout: a part outside the bounds, two parts claiming one cell, a part whose grid unit differs from the collection's. Baseplates contend only with baseplates, on the grid; every other part contends with every other in grid cells and height units, a box claiming its height plus 4.4 mm for its lip. A part lands with the corner of its footprint, turned by its rotation, on its cell, so a trimmed part keeps its trimmed corner where the layout puts it. Items with identical settings share one design.

Dimensions

The parts keep the web generator's dimensions, which keep the Gridfinity profiles but leave a wider gap between bins

The standard leaves half a millimetre between neighbouring bins; the web generator leaves 0.62 mm, and so do these parts. Everything else follows the Gridfinity profiles: a foot that steps in at 45 degrees, straight and at 45 degrees again, a lip that takes the foot of the bin stacked on it, and a baseplate pocket that the foot sits in.

Construction

A part's outer shape is one closed shell sewn from faces, and booleans run only where faces cross

SolidCraft's tapered cuts across a grid of feet are slow and fail on larger grids, so no part is cut out of a block. Each face of the shell is a flat patch, a straight extrusion or a ruled loft between two loops, and the faces are stitched into one solid.

Profiles

A profile of inset and height points swept around a rounded rectangle gives a ring of faces, one per step

A step with no rise is a flat ring, a step with no change of inset a straight wall, and any other step a ruled loft between the rectangle moved inward by the two insets; the corners stay concentric, so a 45 degree step is a true 45 degree face. All outlines swept with one profile share one sketch per point, so the feet of a bin take one sketch per height. The foot, the bin's wall and lip, and the baseplate's pockets are each such a sweep.

Edges

Where a face meets an edge broken into pieces, the face across it breaks at the same points

Stitching joins faces along shared edges, and an edge that one face holds whole and another holds in pieces leaves a solid that later booleans fail on. Three places break edges to match: a bin's outer wall breaks its bottom edge where the feet meet it and where the underside between them does; a baseplate's outer wall breaks its top edge where the pockets meet it; a lifted baseplate's pocket loops break where the pads' edges meet the pocket walls, from the pads all the way up.

Booleans

Elements are drawn across their line, extruded along it, trimmed to the middle of the outer wall by one cut and joined to the bin in one join

The trim reaches every element and keeps its name, and the join takes them all at once. Connector slots are cut into the stitched plate, and a trimmed plate is cut back with a box. A join SolidCraft completes only on a tolerance retry is accepted only where a test holds its volume to a hand calculation: a scoop meeting the floor, and a ledge on an edge line running into the corners.

Running

A part is written to files headless, or built in the open app as a design of its own

# a 3 × 2 bin, 6 units tall, corner magnets, two dividers, a scoop and a label ledge
uv run gridfinity box --units 3 2 --height 6 --magnets corner \
    --wall 12,0,24,y --wall 24,0,24,y --scoop 0,0,36 --ledge 0,24,36,flip \
    --out box-3-2-6.stl --out box-3-2-6.step

# the same settings as JSON, to keep or edit, then built from the file
uv run gridfinity box --units 3 2 --height 6 --print-config > bin.json
uv run gridfinity box --config bin.json --out bin.3mf

uv run gridfinity baseplate --units 5 4 --magnets full --connectable --out plate.stl
uv run gridfinity baseplate --units 7 5 --size 290 200 --out drawer-plate.stl
uv run gridfinity clip --tolerance 0.05 --out clip.stl

# every part into a folder, and the collection design linking them
uv run gridfinity collection --config examples/drawer.json --folder drawer --export stl

# build in the running SolidCraft app instead, in a new tab, and save it
uv run gridfinity box --units 2 1 --live --out box-2-1-3.solidcraft

A file's extension picks its format: STL, STEP, 3MF and OBJ are exports, a .solidcraft name saves the design and .json saves it as plain JSON. With neither an output nor a script named, a part is written as STL under the web generator's name for it. --script also writes the command script, which solidcraft-cli run and the MCP batch tool take unchanged. Building in the app opens a new tab first and leaves whatever is open untouched; the app answers only between frames, and has been seen to stall while its window was in the background.

Extending

General SolidCraft scripting and the Gridfinity parts live in separate packages, and only the second knows what a bin is

PackageModuleHolds
solidcraft_scriptscript.pyThe command list, unique feature and body names, sketch drawing with the ids SolidCraft will give each curve, sketch planes
solidcraft_scriptloops.pyLines, arcs, circles and rounded rectangles; outlines broken at given points; rounding a polyline's corner
solidcraft_scriptshells.pyShells sewn from surfaces: profile sweeps, walls with broken edges, stepped round holes, stitching
solidcraft_scriptrunner.py, control.pyRunning a script with solidcraft-cli and reading back its bodies; driving the running app over its control port
gridfinitystandard.py, config.pyEvery dimension in the tables above; every setting with its range, and reading and writing settings as JSON
gridfinitybase.py, profiles.py, box.py, elements.pyFeet and their holes; the inner face that a lip and a wall leave together; the bin; walls, ledges and scoops
gridfinitybaseplate.py, clip.py, collection.py, cli.pyThe other parts, collection layout and its checks, and the command line

A new element kind is a member of the element kinds, a cross-section drawn in its plane across the line, and a test that measures what it adds against a volume worked out by hand. A new part kind is a settings class that checks its own ranges, a builder that returns a command script, and a subcommand. Dimensions shared by several parts belong in the standard, where one change reaches every part.

The tests come in two layers: geometry, settings and layout checked without SolidCraft, and parts built headless whose volume, size and shell count are measured against values worked out from the dimensions alone. The second layer needs solidcraft-cli on the path and is skipped without it. The doc's figure is drawn from the same geometry by docs/figures.py, and a test fails while the page holds a stale one.

Left out

Cutouts and the web generator's account features are not built

  • Cutouts. SolidCraft runs no boolean on an imported mesh, and an STL subtracted exactly needs a mesh boolean outside it; the first open question decides it.
  • GLB export, the build-plate preview, measurements in inches, and saved models, accounts and the model library.

Re-plan triggers

Three questions change what gets built: cutouts, the feet of trimmed bins and the gap between bins
